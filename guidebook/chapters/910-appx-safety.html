A checklist of rules that protect the board, your device and you. Most of them come from experience of things going wrong.

B.1 Protecting the RFSoC 4x2

  • Attenuate first. Never connect a signal of unknown power to an ADC input. Start with 20 dB of attenuation and remove it only after measuring. Respect the maximum input ratings in AMD's DS926 .
  • No DC on the SMA ports. Use DC blocks when connecting to anything that may carry DC (bias tees, amplifier outputs during power-up, fridge lines with bias).
  • ESD. Wear a grounded wrist strap when touching the board or connecting cables; discharge cables before connecting. QICK's quick-start guide warns that touching exposed components with metal tools can cause immediate, permanent damage .
  • Power and cooling. Use the supplied 12 V, 10 A supply; never block the fan; watch the FAIL and OVER TEMP LEDs .
  • Logic levels. PMOD pins are 1.8 V logic; use level shifters, never connect 3.3 V or 5 V outputs to them .
  • Shut down cleanly before switching off, to protect the SD card; keep a spare, imaged card.
  • Network security. Change the default xilinx password if the board is on a shared network.

B.2 Protecting the quantum device

  • Gate limits. Set software voltage limits and ramp rates on every gate; log the maximum each gate has seen.
  • Grounding. Ground all device lines (with a breakout box or grounding caps) when connecting or disconnecting anything; un-ground only when everything is connected.
  • Ground loops. Connect instrument grounds at one point; use isolation where needed (Chapter 16).
  • Microwave power. Increase EDSR and readout power gradually while watching mixing-chamber temperature and sensor peak width.
  • Pulse amplitudes. Before applying a new pulse sequence, check its amplitude at room temperature with an oscilloscope and compute the amplitude at the device from your attenuation.

B.3 Around the cryostat

  • Superconducting magnets store large energies; follow your lab's procedures for ramping and quench protection, and keep magnetic tools away.
  • Cryogens and pressurised gas: dry dilution refrigerators still contain helium mixtures and cold surfaces; follow the manufacturer's and your lab's safety training.
  • Vibration and cables: secure cables so they do not move with the pulse tube; moving cables generate noise.

B.4 Before every new experiment

  1. Is the frequency plan written, and are filters in place for every converter input?
  2. Is the power budget computed from DAC to device and from device to ADC?
  3. Are all instruments locked to the same 10 MHz reference?
  4. Are trigger latencies and time of flight calibrated?
  5. Is the configuration (firmware, software versions, calibrations) saved with the data?
