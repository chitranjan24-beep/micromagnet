A from-zero guidebook

The RFSoC 4x2 for Semiconductor Spin Qubits

Quantum dots, spin qubits, signals, FPGAs, PYNQ and QICK, explained from the inside out

This book is written for someone who has undergraduate physics, maths and solid-state physics, and who is new to FPGAs, software-defined radio, PYNQ, QICK and to how a quantum-dot or spin-qubit lab actually works. It starts from the beginning of each topic and builds up until you can design, run and understand the experiments that research groups run on Si/SiGe and GaAs quantum-dot devices, and see exactly which parts of that work an RFSoC 4x2 board can do.

Version 1.0, October 2026. Hardware facts from the RFSoC 4x2 reference manual (rev. A6); software facts from QICK 0.2.436 and spinQICK 3.0.0.

Use the contents list on the left (or the Contents button on a phone) to move between chapters. The search box finds section headings and defined terms. Every chapter ends with links to the previous and next chapter.

The map of the book

How the chapters are built

Every chapter follows the same pattern, so you always know where you are:

  1. Why you need this. One short box that ties the chapter to a real task in the lab.
  2. The picture. An analogy and a diagram before any equations.
  3. The precise idea. The physics or engineering, with every symbol explained and no skipped steps.
  4. On the 4x2. Where the idea shows up on the board, in PYNQ or in QICK.
  5. Worked examples. Numbers you can check with a calculator, or short code.
  6. Common mistakes. What goes wrong in real labs.
  7. Check yourself. Questions with hidden answers you can open.
  8. Summary. The few lines to remember.

Sources

Board numbers come from the RFSoC-PYNQ board page and the Real Digital reference manual , QICK numbers from the QICK source code and documentation as of October 2026 , and the signal-processing background follows AMD's free RFSoC SDR book . Where sources disagree, the text says so.

About the SDR book file

The SDR book's licence allows free use for non-profit teaching and research, but it says the PDF must only be distributed from the book's own website and must not be hosted on third-party sites. If the GitHub repository holding RFSoC_SDR_book.zip is public, consider removing the zip and linking to rfsocbook.com instead.
