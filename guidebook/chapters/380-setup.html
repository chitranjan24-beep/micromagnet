Why you need this

Every experiment in this part needs a concrete hardware plan: which signal comes from which instrument, through which components, at what power, triggered by what. Because the 4x2 has two AC-coupled outputs, the plan decides what you can do. This chapter gives three architectures, from "use the board as it is" to "move to a larger board", with wiring, frequency plans, power budgets and parts lists, so you can choose consciously and know what each choice costs.

The constraints, restated

  • 2 DAC outputs (DAC_A, DAC_B), 4 ADC inputs (QICK standard firmware reads ADC_C and ADC_D), all AC-coupled via 10 MHz–10 GHz baluns .
  • Direct synthesis to ~4.9 GHz (zone 1), with reduced power to ~9.8 GHz (zone 2); ADC zone 1 to ~2.2 GHz .
  • 8 timed trigger outputs (1.8 V), external 10 MHz reference input.
  • A Si/SiGe micromagnet device: many DC gates, PSB or Elzerman readout, EDSR at roughly 10–20 GHz at typical fields (Chapter 12).

Architecture A: the 4x2 as readout, microwave and timing engine (no modification)

What it can do

Everything in Chapters 39–42: reflectometry readout, fast charge-stability maps (with the AWG or DC source sweeping and the 4x2 recording), PSB and Elzerman readout (AWG pulses, 4x2 measuring), EDSR spectroscopy, Rabi, Ramsey, echo, AllXY and randomised benchmarking, plus real-time frequency tracking. Two-qubit gates are possible if the AWG has enough channels for plungers and barrier.

What it costs

  • A DC-coupled AWG with 2–4 channels and trigger input (or an integrated controller's baseband channels).
  • A microwave LO and mixer components for EDSR above ~9.8 GHz.
  • Calibration of trigger latency and jitter between the 4x2 and the AWG (Chapter 36).

An example power budget for the readout

DAC_B at about −10 dBm; −20 dB at room temperature, −20 dB at 4 K, −10 dB at the still, −20 dB at the mixing chamber and a −20 dB coupler gives −100 dBm at the tank. The reflected signal (−100 dBm or a bit less) passes the coupler's through path, a HEMT (+35 dB), a room-temperature amplifier (+30 dB), and arrives at ADC_D around −35 dBm (Chapter 18). Add a low-pass filter before ADC_D to stop the EDSR IF and other signals from aliasing.

Architecture B: a modified 4x2 for baseband (advanced)

Make one or both DAC outputs DC-coupled (remove the output balun and capacitors and provide correct termination and a DC-coupled differential amplifier, Chapter 29). Then QICK plays plunger pulses directly. Since both DACs are then gate channels, the readout carrier must come from elsewhere: an external RF source locked to the same 10 MHz, with the 4x2's readout block demodulating it. The demodulated phase is then stable but has an arbitrary offset, which the IQ rotation removes. This architecture can run a singlet–triplet qubit (two fast gates plus readout) on a single 4x2. It carries real risk to the board, and none of the sources used for this book documents such a modification of the 4x2's DAC outputs.

Architecture C: grow into a ZCU216 or QICK Box

The ZCU216 has 16 DACs and 16 ADCs on connectors that can be AC- or DC-coupled, and it is the platform of spinQICK and the published RFSoC spin-qubit work . The QICK Box adds a modular front end with RF, balun and DC-coupled modules . In this architecture the 4x2 remains valuable as a development, teaching and test board (same FPGA fabric and the same QICK software model ), or as a dedicated readout and microwave unit.

In the lab

Parts list for Architecture A beyond the 4x2: SMA attenuators (3, 10, 20 dB), DC blocks, SMA low-pass and band-pass filters for the readout and IF bands, a room-temperature low-noise amplifier for the readout, a directional coupler and HEMT in the fridge (often already present), a microwave LO, a mixer and filter for EDSR, a PMOD level shifter and BNC adapters for triggers, a 10 MHz distribution amplifier, and short, good-quality SMA cables. Budget time to characterise each component.

Check yourself

1. In Architecture A, why must the EDSR IF be kept out of ADC_D?

Show answer
Any IF leakage into the readout input is digitised; at 1.5 GHz it lies in the ADC's first zone, and its harmonics alias across the band. It can add spurs near the readout carrier or saturate the input. A low-pass filter on ADC_D (cut-off above the readout carrier, below the IF) prevents this.

2. Why does Architecture B need an external readout source?

Show answer
Both DACs are used for DC-coupled gate pulses, leaving no DAC for the readout carrier. The ADC side can still demodulate an external carrier if it is locked to the same reference.
Summary
  • A: unmodified 4x2 for readout, EDSR (direct or via mixer) and timing, with a DC-coupled AWG and DC source triggered by PMOD; the recommended start.
  • B: DC-coupled DAC modification for baseband pulses; readout carrier from an external source; risky.
  • C: ZCU216/QICK Box with spinQICK for EO and multi-qubit work; 4x2 as test or readout unit.
  • Write a frequency plan and a power budget, filter the ADC input, and lock everything to one 10 MHz reference.
