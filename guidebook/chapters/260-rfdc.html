Why you need this

The RF data converters are the part of the RFSoC that touches the analog world. They are organised into tiles with their own clocks and PLLs, they have built-in mixers, filters and calibration engines, and they are configured from software through an interface called the RF Data Converter (RFDC) driver. QICK sets them up for you, but when you check power levels, debug a missing signal, add an external clock, or read a QICK printout that says "DAC tile 2, blk 0 is DAC_A", you need this map. This chapter summarises the SDR book's three data-converter chapters and AMD's PG269 product guide for the parts that matter to you .

Tiles and blocks

The converters are grouped into tiles. Each tile has its own clock input and PLL, and contains two or four converter blocks, each with its own digital signal-processing pipeline. In AMD's naming, ADC tiles are numbered 224–227 and DAC tiles 228–231. In software (PYNQ's xrfdc and QICK) the same tiles are numbered 0–3 within each type.

On the RFSoC 4x2, the XCZU48DR has 8 ADCs and 8 DACs, of which the board brings out 4 ADCs and 2 DACs :

The RF-ADC pipeline

  • Input buffer and digital step attenuator (DSA): Gen3 devices can attenuate the input in steps before conversion, useful if your signal is too strong.
  • The ADC core: 14 bits at up to 5 GSPS on Gen3, built from interleaved sub-converters with background calibration (Chapter 22).
  • Threshold detectors: flag over-range inputs.
  • Complex mixer: a coarse mixer (shifts by \(f_s/4\), \(f_s/2\)) and a fine mixer with a 48-bit NCO, converting real input to complex baseband.
  • Decimation filters: 1× to 40× on Gen3.
  • FIFO and AXI-Stream: hand the samples to the fabric at the fabric clock rate.

The RF-DAC pipeline

Clocks and PLLs

Each tile needs a sampling clock. It can come from an external clock pin directly, or from the tile's internal PLL multiplying a lower reference. On the 4x2, external LMX2594 synthesisers feed the tiles (Chapter 30), and the QICK firmware configures the tiles with a 491.52 MHz reference, generating 9830.4 MSPS for the DACs and 4423.68 MSPS for the ADCs . Because different tiles have different PLLs, their sampling clocks can have different phases after power-up. AMD's multi-tile synchronisation (MTS) feature aligns tiles to each other, and QICK's documentation describes how clocks are grouped ("Groups of related clocks: [tProc clock, DAC tile 0, DAC tile 2], [ADC tile 0]" in the 4x2 printout) .

Configuration from software

The RFDC is configured through the xrfdc driver, which PYNQ exposes in Python: you can read and set Nyquist zones, mixer frequencies, decimation and interpolation, DSA and VOP settings, and check PLL lock status. QICK wraps the settings it needs, for example set_nyquist() (Chapter 19) and the DAC current mode, and leaves the rest at the firmware's defaults. Changing a converter's sampling rate is possible but involves reprogramming clocks; QICK's documentation has a dedicated page on it .

Common mistake

Changing RFDC settings directly with xrfdc while a QICK program runs, or changing them behind QICK's back. QICK caches some settings (Nyquist zone, mixer frequency) and assumes it controls them. Make changes through QICK's methods, or reload the firmware afterwards.

Check yourself

1. A QICK printout says "ADC tile 0, blk 0 is ADC_D". Which AMD tile number is that, and which SMA connector?

Show answer
ADC tile 224 (software index 0), the SMA labelled ADC_D on the board.

2. Why does QICK not use the converters' built-in mixers and decimators on the 4x2?

Show answer
It needs per-pulse, phase-coherent control of frequency and phase by the tProcessor, and several independent readout configurations. Doing synthesis and down-conversion in the fabric gives that flexibility; the hardened DUC/DDC are configured from software and are less suited to changing every pulse.
Summary
  • Converters are grouped into tiles with their own clocks and PLLs; ADC tiles 224–227, DAC tiles 228–231 (software 0–3).
  • On the 4x2: DAC_A = tile 230, DAC_B = tile 228, ADC_A/B = tile 226, ADC_C/D = tile 224; QICK standard firmware uses DAC_B, DAC_A, ADC_D, ADC_C.
  • ADC path: buffer/DSA, ADC, threshold, QMC, mixer, decimation; DAC path: interpolation, mixer, QMC/IMR/inverse sinc, DAC, output mode.
  • QICK sets 1× rates and does DSP in fabric; tile clocks are aligned with multi-tile synchronisation.
