The mathematics used in this book, in one place, with the identities you will reach for most often.

A.1 Complex numbers and phasors

  • \(z = x + iy = re^{i\theta}\), with \(r = |z| = \sqrt{x^2+y^2}\) and \(\theta = \operatorname{atan2}(y, x)\).
  • Euler: \(e^{i\theta} = \cos\theta + i\sin\theta\); so \(\cos\theta = (e^{i\theta}+e^{-i\theta})/2\), \(\sin\theta = (e^{i\theta}-e^{-i\theta})/2i\).
  • Multiplying complex numbers multiplies magnitudes and adds angles: rotating a phasor by \(\phi\) means multiplying by \(e^{i\phi}\).
  • A sinusoid \(A\cos(\omega t+\phi) = \Re\{Ae^{i\phi}e^{i\omega t}\} = I\cos\omega t - Q\sin\omega t\) with \(I + iQ = Ae^{i\phi}\).

A.2 Trigonometric identities used for mixing and the RWA

\[ \cos A\cos B = \tfrac12[\cos(A-B)+\cos(A+B)],\quad \sin A\sin B = \tfrac12[\cos(A-B)-\cos(A+B)],\quad \sin A\cos B = \tfrac12[\sin(A+B)+\sin(A-B)]. \]

A.4 Linear algebra for qubits

  • Hermitian: \(H = H^\dagger\) (real eigenvalues, observables). Unitary: \(U^\dagger U = I\) (evolutions, gates).
  • Pauli matrices: \(\sigma_i^2 = I\), \(\sigma_x\sigma_y = i\sigma_z\) (cyclic), \(\{\sigma_i,\sigma_j\} = 2\delta_{ij}I\).
  • \(e^{-i\theta\,\hat n\cdot\vec\sigma/2} = \cos(\theta/2)I - i\sin(\theta/2)\,\hat n\cdot\vec\sigma\).
  • Tensor products: \((A\otimes B)(\ket a\otimes\ket b) = A\ket a\otimes B\ket b\); two-qubit basis \(\ket{00},\ket{01},\ket{10},\ket{11}\).
  • Second-order perturbation theory (used for exchange): \(E_n^{(2)} = \sum_{m\ne n}|V_{mn}|^2/(E_n - E_m)\).

A.5 Statistics

  • Binomial estimate of a probability from \(N\) shots: \(\sigma = \sqrt{P(1-P)/N}\).
  • Gaussian overlap error with threshold midway, SNR = \(\Delta\mu/\sigma\): \(\epsilon = \tfrac12\operatorname{erfc}(\text{SNR}/2\sqrt2)\).
  • Averaging \(N\) independent samples reduces white noise by \(\sqrt N\).
  • Gaussian average: \(\ev{\cos(\delta\omega\,\tau)} = e^{-\sigma^2\tau^2/2}\) for \(\delta\omega\sim\mathcal N(0,\sigma^2)\).

A.6 Decibels

  • Power ratio: \(10\log_{10}(P_2/P_1)\); voltage ratio: \(20\log_{10}(V_2/V_1)\).
  • dBm: \(10\log_{10}(P/1\ \text{mW})\); in 50 Ω, 0 dBm = 0.224 V RMS = 0.316 V amplitude.
  • Thermal noise: \(-174\) dBm/Hz at 290 K; add \(10\log_{10}B\) for bandwidth \(B\).
