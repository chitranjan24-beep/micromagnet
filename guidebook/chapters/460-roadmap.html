Why you need this

A plan turns this book into results. This chapter lays out a realistic sequence of stages, from reading to a working qubit measurement, with clear goals and checks for each stage, and a list of projects that suit the 4x2 and could become research contributions in their own right.

Stage 0: before (or while) the board arrives

  • Read Parts I–III of this book; then Hanson et al. and Burkard et al. for depth .
  • Read the QICK documentation's getting-started and tutorial pages; if available, try the tProc v2 tutorials on the QICK emulator branch .
  • Read the SDR book's Chapters 4, 7, 9, 11 and 12 (sampling, quadrature, RF converters, figures of merit) .
  • Check: you can explain Figure 1.2, a Rabi chevron, a charge stability diagram, a Nyquist zone and an I/Q demodulator without notes.

Stage 1: board bring-up (week 1–2)

  • Flash the QICK-recommended PYNQ image, boot, connect, run the base-overlay RF notebooks (Chapter 31).
  • Install the SDR book notebooks; work through sets A, B, C, E, F (Chapter 32).
  • Record your board's DAC output spectra and a DAC→ADC loopback transfer function (Chapters 29 and 36).
  • Check: a saved "board health" notebook with plots you can compare against later.

Stage 2: QICK on the bench (week 3–4)

  • Install QICK; run the bundled tProc v1 demos; build or obtain tProc v2 firmware and run tutorials 00–04 (Chapter 34).
  • Do the bench calibrations: time of flight, frequency matching, phase versus frequency, trigger latency to an AWG (Chapter 36).
  • Lock the board to a 10 MHz reference together with an LO; build and characterise your EDSR upconversion chain into a spectrum analyser (Chapters 20, 30, 42).
  • Check: loopback pulses at the expected times, stable I/Q phase across runs, a clean upconverted tone with image and LO leakage measured.

Stage 3: reflectometry rehearsal (month 2)

  • Build a room-temperature dummy tank and run the full Chapter 39 procedure on it: resonance, matching, power, SNR versus integration time.
  • Write your frequency plan and power budget for the real fridge (Chapters 18, 22, 38).
  • Check: a measured \(t_{\min}\) on the dummy tank and a written plan reviewed by a senior lab member.

Stage 4: on the device (month 3 onward)

  • Connect to the fridge with conservative attenuation; find the cold resonance; read the sensor dot; measure Coulomb peaks by reflectometry and compare with DC (Chapter 39).
  • Fast charge stability maps with the AWG or DC source sweeping (Chapter 40); calibrate virtual gates.
  • Tunnelling records with the DDR4 buffer; tunnel rates.
  • Check: a sub-second CSD of the qubit dots in the few-electron regime.

Stage 5: spins

  • PSB or Elzerman readout with the AWG for plunger pulses (Chapter 41); single-shot fidelity; \(T_1\) versus field.
  • EDSR spectroscopy, Rabi, Ramsey, echo, AllXY, RB (Chapter 42).
  • Check: a calibrated single-spin qubit with reported \(T_1\), \(T_2^*\), \(T_2\), readout and gate fidelities.

Stage 6: decide how to grow

With a working single-qubit setup, decide whether you need DC-coupled channels on the RFSoC itself (modification, ZCU216/QICK Box with spinQICK) or a commercial controller for multi-qubit and encoded-qubit work (Chapters 38 and 45). Keep the 4x2 as a readout or development unit.

In the lab

Keep a lab notebook (electronic) for every session with: firmware and software versions, calibration values, wiring changes, device state and what you tried. Commit your measurement code to version control. Share results with the QICK and spinQICK communities: they are small, active and helpful, and your experience with the 4x2 on spin qubits is exactly what they lack.

Summary
  • Stage 0–2: learn, bring up the board, run QICK on the bench with calibrations.
  • Stage 3: rehearse reflectometry at room temperature; plan frequencies and power.
  • Stage 4–5: readout, fast maps, spin readout and single-qubit control on the device.
  • Stage 6: choose how to scale; meanwhile, pursue 4x2-friendly projects like real-time frequency tracking and optimal readout.
