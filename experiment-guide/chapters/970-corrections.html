This book replaces an earlier, shorter experimental guide. That guide had the right overall structure but contained a number of errors, some small and some that would mislead in the lab. They are listed here, with the correct statement and the chapter where the topic is treated, so that anyone who used the earlier guide can check what to unlearn. Section numbers refer to the earlier guide.

WhereEarlier statementCorrect statementCh.
Fridge (1.1)The still provides the primary cooling.Cooling happens in the mixing chamber, where 3He crosses from the concentrated to the dilute phase. The still's evaporation drives the circulation.4
Cooldown (1.2)Leak-check and pump the IVC.Dry (pulse-tube) fridges have no inner vacuum can; the outer vacuum can is pumped.4
Shielding (1.5)Mu-metal and lead shields around the sample.With a superconducting magnet at 0.1–1 T, mu-metal saturates and a superconducting shield would block or trap the applied field. Magnetic shielding of this kind is not used at the sample.4
Readout amplifier (1.4.3)A 4–8 GHz HEMT for reflectometry.Reflectometry runs at roughly 100–500 MHz; the cryogenic amplifier must cover that band.4, 10
Reflectometry wiring (2.1, 5.2.3)Directional coupler at room temperature.The coupler must be cold, near the device. Otherwise the weak reflected signal passes back through the input line's attenuators before reaching the amplifier.4, 10
AWG resolution (2.3)14 bits gives 10 µV on ±1 V.14 bits over 2 V gives 122 µV per step; 10 µV needs about 18 bits.5
DC charge sensing (5.1)Bandwidth about 1 MHz.The sensor's ~50–100 kΩ with nanofarads of cable and filter capacitance limits the bandwidth to kilohertz. This is the reason rf reflectometry (or cold amplification) is needed.9
Coulomb peaks (4.1)ΔV = e/(αCg).ΔV = e/Cg = EC/(eα) (plus the orbital spacing where relevant).7, 8
Elzerman readout (5.3)B > 0.04 T (Si) and 0.1 T (GaAs) for EZ > 5kBT at 100 mK.EZ > 5kBT at 100 mK needs B > 0.37 T in Si and about 1.7 T in GaAs; good readout needs more, EZ ≳ 13kBTe. The original experiment used 10 T.14, 15
Elzerman readout (5.3)Γ ≪ 1/tmeas.The reverse: the readout window must be much longer than 1/Γ, while Γ must be slower than the sensor bandwidth so that each event is resolved.15
Readout time (2.8 vs 5.3)1–10 µs in one place, 1–10 ms in another.The sensor integration time (µs) and the readout window (hundreds of µs to ms for Elzerman readout) are different quantities.15, 17
ESR versus EDSR (6.3)1 mT of B1 gives a 14 kHz Rabi frequency.It gives about 14 MHz (fR = gµBB1/2h). The argument built on the wrong number does not hold.18
EDSR (6.3.2)δx = eαV/(m*ω0²).The displacement is set by the electric field at the dot, δx = eE/(m*ω0²), not by the lever-arm energy.18
Exchange-only qubit (8.1–8.3)The Sz = ½ subspace is two-dimensional; the quartet is excluded by energy.It is three-dimensional. The S = 3/2 state is excluded by spin symmetry: exchange conserves total spin.28
Exchange-only qubit (8.2)Formula for the two rotation axes.J12 rotates about z, J23 about an axis 120° away.28
Exchange-only qubit (8.3)Ramp "through (1,0,1)".(1,0,1) holds two electrons, not three; the three-electron configurations are (1,1,1), (2,0,1), (1,0,2) and so on.28
Ramsey and echo (11.2–11.3)Charge noise gives exponential decay; 1/f noise gives an echo exponent of 3.Quasi-static noise gives Gaussian Ramsey decay; 1/f noise gives an echo exponent near 2; an exponent of 3 corresponds to 1/f² noise.20, 21
Randomized benchmarking (11.5)ε = (1 − p)/2 · (1 − 1/d).r = (1 − p)(d − 1)/d, which is (1 − p)/2 for one qubit.23
Noise spectroscopy (12.3)S(f0) = π/T2*.No such relation holds. CPMG gives S at f0 = N/(2t) from −ln W ≈ 8S(f0)t (one-sided, Hz²/Hz).21
Hybrid systems (10.1)Mi 2018 and Samkharadze 2018 did two-qubit gates through a resonator.Those papers demonstrated strong spin–photon coupling. Resonator-mediated spin–spin interaction was shown by Borjans et al. in 2020.29
Device tuning (throughout)"Make barriers more negative", "sweep the plunger more negative to empty the dot".That is GaAs depletion-mode logic. In undoped Si/SiGe accumulation devices the polarities are reversed: more positive gates add electrons and open barriers.6, 7
ReferencesPioro-Ladrière 2008 listed as a silicon experiment.It was a GaAs experiment. Several other references in the earlier guide also needed correction; the reference list of this book was checked against the published records.refs

Beyond individual errors, the earlier guide was missing several topics that matter for silicon micromagnet devices, all of which are now covered: accumulation-mode turn-on (Chapter 6), electron temperature (Chapters 4, 8), valley splitting (Chapter 14), micromagnet trade-offs (Chapters 2, 14), singlet–triplet versus parity blockade readout (Chapter 16), heating and frequency drift (Chapters 20, 22), bias-tee droop and pulse distortion (Chapter 13), calibration procedures (Chapters 22, 31), exchange calibration and the choice of two-qubit gates (Chapters 24, 25), error budgets (Chapter 32) and shuttling (Chapter 29).
