All the "When it goes wrong" boxes of the book in one table, in the order the problems tend to appear in an experiment. Each row gives what you see, the most likely causes, and the chapter where the problem and its fixes are discussed.

What you seeLikely causesWhere
At the first cold measurement, one gate draws current as soon as it is raised by a few hundred millivolts, or two gates behave as if they were one.Oxide breakdown, often from an electrostatic discharge during bonding or connection, or from a voltage step applied without a slow ramp.Ch. 3
Charge transitions and Coulomb peaks are much broader than expected, and a Fermi-function fit gives Te of 150 to 300 mK although the mixing chamber is at 10 mK.Ch. 4
Raising gate B2 makes a current appear on gate P1, proportional to the B2 voltage.A resistive short between B2 and P1 through the oxide separating their layers, which would show up as a resistance of perhaps megaohms between the two lines.Ch. 6
No current at any gate voltage, even well above the expected threshold.Ch. 6
In the barrier–barrier map, there are resonances, but they respond to only one barrier, or run at odd angles, or there are many overlapping sets of them.Unintended dots formed by disorder, typically under one of the barriers, or a double dot where you expected a single dot.Ch. 7
The diamonds do not close cleanly. Their edges are broken into segments, or extra diamonds of different sizes overlap.More than one dot in series, often a disorder dot under a barrier in addition to the intended one. Two dots in series produce a pattern of overlapping or irregular diamonds instead of a regular chain.Ch. 8
The sensor signal occasionally jumps by a large amount and stays there, or its sensitivity changes from hour to hour.A charge trap near the sensor switching state. That shifts the sensor peak by a fraction of its width, exactly like a real electron would. Slow drift of the gate voltages or of trapped charges has a similar, gradual effect.Ch. 9
The reflected signal barely changes when the sensor is swept through its Coulomb peaks, although the DC current shows the peaks clearly.The tank is badly mismatched, so the sensor resistance is far from L/(Z₀Cp); the carrier frequency is not at the resonance; the carrier is too weak or far too strong; or the reflected signal is lost through a broken output line or a missing amplifier bias.Ch. 10
Near the apparent (0,0) region, the transition lines become blurred or jagged and then fade out, instead of ending cleanly.The dot–reservoir tunnel rate has become slower than the sweep. The electron is "latched" on or off the dot, missing transitions.Ch. 11
The PAT map shows no resonance, although the sensor clearly sees the interdot transition.The microwaves are not reaching the gate (a broken or badly attenuated line, a bias tee that blocks high frequencies, a bad bond); the power is far too low; or the tunnel coupling is so large that the gap 2tc/h lies above your frequency range.Ch. 12
A pulsed experiment works for the first few seconds after you start it and then fades, or a readout point that worked yesterday is off by a few millivolts today, after the sequence was changed.The sequence's average is not zero, and the bias tee is shifting every level by it. Changing the length of any stage changes the average, and with it the shift.Ch. 13
Lines in a video-mode map appear doubled, or shifted between the forward and backward sweeps.A delay between the ramp and the digitizer.Ch. 13
T1 is only a few hundred microseconds at a field where you expected tens of milliseconds, and it gets dramatically worse as the field is raised.A small valley splitting, so that the operating field sits near the hotspot.Ch. 14
No spin bump in the read-level scan, only the sharp step between "always empty" and "never empty".The electron temperature is too high for this EZ, so the window is washed out; the tunnel rates are too fast, so the bump lasts less than the sensor can resolve, or too slow, so it does not finish within the read stage; or the read pulse is not the amplitude you think (Chapter 13).Ch. 15
No blockade strip, although the interdot transition looks normal.EST is very small, because dot 2 has a small valley splitting; the readout point lies outside the narrow strip; the triplets are lost in the pulse, for example through the S–T− crossing; or the integration is far longer than the blockade lifetime.Ch. 16
The visibility drops from 98% to 85% over an afternoon, although nothing was changed.The sensor has drifted off its operating point, so the peaks have moved relative to the fixed threshold; or the readout level (Elzerman) or readout point (blockade) has drifted with a slow charge rearrangement.Ch. 17
No resonance anywhere in the search range.The microwaves do not reach the gate; the qubit frequency is outside the range, because the micromagnet field differs more from the simulation than expected or the magnet is not saturated; the burst arrives while the dot is not in the manipulation configuration (a timing error, Chapter 13); or the readout is not working and would not see a flip anyway.Ch. 18
The chevron is lopsided, with fringes bending more on one side, and the centre of the chevron moves when the burst gets longer.The qubit frequency shifts during the burst, typically because the microwave power heats the device. Pulse distortion that changes the dot's position during the burst has a similar signature.Ch. 19
The Rabi frequency does not scale linearly with amplitude, even at low power.Saturation or compression in the microwave chain, often an amplifier or the IQ mixer driven too hard.Ch. 19
The Ramsey fringes are clean on one run and much shorter-lived on the next, or the fringe frequency changes between consecutive runs.Slow frequency jumps, often a charge trap near the dot switching between two states. Each run then catches one or both frequencies.Ch. 20
CPMG with many pulses gives a shorter coherence than with fewer pulses, or the coherence oscillates with τ.Pulse errors accumulating, because each π pulse is slightly off in amplitude or frequency. CPMG is robust to such errors only for the state along the pulse axis; for other states, or with the wrong phase convention, errors add up. Oscillations at specific spacings can also come from residual 29Si nuclei: the filter's peak hitting the nuclear Larmor frequency shows up as a sharp dip in coherence.Ch. 21
Each calibration converges, but a gate sequence built from the calibrated gates performs worse than expected, and worse for longer sequences.The calibration and the experiment run under different conditions, typically a different microwave duty cycle, so heating shifts the frequency between them. Phase crosstalk that was not included in the calibration does the same.Ch. 22
The RB decay is not a clean exponential: it bends down at long sequence lengths, or the spread between random sequences is much larger than shot noise.Errors that change during a sequence, most often heating that accumulates with the number of pulses, or drift of the qubit frequency during the hours of the measurement. A large spread between sequences means the errors are coherent and do not average out, typically a residual miscalibration.Ch. 23
J cannot be turned off: even at the most negative barrier you can safely apply, the conditional splitting stays above 200 kHz.The dots are too close or too large; the barrier gate has a weak lever arm on the tunnel coupling; or the dots are not at the symmetry point but tilted, where J is larger.Ch. 24
The CZ conditional phase is calibrated to π, but a sequence of several CZ gates goes wrong quickly, and the error depends on how long ago the last calibration was.J drifts, through slow charge rearrangement near the barrier, and with it the conditional phase. Or heating from the barrier pulses shifts the qubit frequencies, so single-qubit phases change with the number of gates.Ch. 25
The Bell-state fidelity is much lower than gate fidelities suggest, and the density matrix shows a strong |01⟩ or |10⟩ population.Imperfect initialization of one qubit, readout crosstalk (reading one qubit disturbs the other), or a population-swapping error in the CZ from too-fast ramps.Ch. 26
The calibration graph runs, every node passes its fit, and yet circuit results are worse than yesterday.A parameter that is not in the graph has drifted, often a crosstalk phase, the heating steady state, or a virtual-gate coefficient after a charge rearrangement; or a check is too lenient and passes a parameter that is slightly off.Ch. 31

Two general rules cover most cases not in the table. First, go back one step: most failures are caused by a parameter from an earlier stage that has drifted or was never quite right, such as a tunnel rate, a sensor point or a pulse amplitude. Second, measure the simplest thing that could distinguish the possible causes, before changing anything.
